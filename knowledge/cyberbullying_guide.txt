CYBERBULLYING GUIDE

What is cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is repeated or targeted harmful behavior carried out through digital platforms such as social media, messaging apps, online games, forums, or other internet services.

Examples of cyberbullying include:

- Repeatedly sending abusive or threatening messages.
- Deliberately targeting someone with insulting comments.
- Sharing someone's private information without permission.
- Creating fake accounts to harass or impersonate someone.
- Spreading harmful rumors about someone online.
- Encouraging other people to participate in harassment.

Important factors to consider:

- Context
- Repetition
- Targeting
- Threats
- Severity
- Power imbalance or coordinated behavior

What should a person do if they experience cyberbullying?

1. Do not respond aggressively.
2. Save screenshots and other evidence.
3. Block or mute the person when appropriate.
4. Report abusive content through the platform's reporting system.
5. Tell a trusted parent, teacher, friend, or other responsible person.
6. Protect personal information.
7. Seek additional help when the situation involves serious threats or danger.

AI systems can help identify potentially harmful language or patterns, but they cannot make a final legal determination of whether a situation is cyberbullying.
